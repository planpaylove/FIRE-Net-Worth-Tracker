Same life, five different finish lines. Regular FIRE is the middle path; the others trade spending for years, or years for spending.

How the maths works

Your FIRE number is your annual spending divided by your safe withdrawal rate. At 4%, that is 25× your spending — the portfolio that can pay your life, every year, without running out.

Years to FI comes from your invested portfolio growing at your real return, plus what you save each year (income minus spending), until it reaches your FIRE number.

Three bad days, run against your plan. A plan that only works in good years is not a plan.

What this means

A market crash early on hurts the most, because your portfolio is small and has the longest time to recover from. Saving more each year is the strongest defence — it beats hoping for higher returns.

How the maths works

FIRE number = annual spending ÷ safe withdrawal rate. At 4%, that is 25× spending.

Years to FI: your portfolio grows at the real return and you add your yearly savings (income − spending) until it reaches the FIRE number. Solved exactly, then rounded to whole years on the chart.

Coast FIRE: the portfolio you would need today, growing untouched at the real return, to reach your FIRE number by your target age. If you already have it, you are Coast FI — work becomes optional for money, even if you never save another dollar.

Barista FIRE: the FIRE number for the spending your part-time work does not cover — (spending − part-time income) ÷ withdrawal rate.

Stress test: the same yearly simulation with a −30% crash in year one, a lower return, or higher spending — your FI year under each.

This is an educational planning model, not financial, tax, or investment advice. Safe withdrawal rates are historical heuristics, not guarantees. Replace every sample number with your own before you rely on a result.
